📚Key Vocabulary

Anabaptist

A 16th century Christian reform movement that believed only adult believers should be baptised. The Amish grew out of this movement in Europe.

Jakob Ammann

A Swiss-born leader (1690s) who insisted on stricter separation from the world, causing a split from the Mennonites and founding what became the Amish movement.

Ordnung

The unwritten set of rules that govern all areas of Amish community life. They promote humility, simplicity and obedience, and help communities avoid pride.

Rumspringa

A period (usually ages 16–20) when Amish teenagers experience life outside the community before deciding whether to commit to the Amish faith through baptism.

Plain living

A simple lifestyle deliberately kept free of pride, excess and modern luxury. Plain dress, horse-drawn transport and home-based worship all reflect this ideal.

Adult baptism

Baptism that happens only when a person is old enough to freely choose their faith — usually between ages 16 and 24, after Rumspringa.

Persecution

Being treated unfairly or harshly because of your beliefs. The Amish faced persecution in Europe, which led them to emigrate to America in the 1700s.

Separation

The Amish principle of keeping apart from mainstream "worldly" society. It protects the community's faith, values and way of life from outside influences.

Knowledge

The Amish grew from the Anabaptist movement in 16th century Europe. Jakob Ammann led a stricter group that split from the Mennonites in the 1690s. The Amish faced persecution in Europe and began moving to America in the early 1700s. Today most Amish live in rural communities in Pennsylvania, Ohio and Indiana. Daily life is guided by the Ordnung, and baptism only happens after teenagers freely choose their faith during Rumspringa.

Sort distinctive Amish practices into the correct areas of daily life.
Knowledge

Beliefs & Worship: The Bible is read literally. Services are held in community members' homes every other Sunday, conducted in Pennsylvania Dutch, lasting around three hours. Adult baptism follows Rumspringa.

Family & Education: Large families are common. Marriages are arranged within the community. Children attend school only to 8th grade, then learn practical trades and domestic skills.

Technology & Transport: Technology is evaluated by whether it strengthens or weakens community. Cars and electricity from the grid are usually avoided; horse-drawn buggies are used for transport.

Clothing & Appearance: Dress is plain, modest and uniform — dark suits and broad-brimmed hats for men, bonnets and long dresses for women. Uniformity shows commitment to community over self.

Knowledge

The Amish do not reject technology for its own sake — they ask whether it supports or threatens their community values of humility, togetherness and separation. Cars and television bring the outside world in or allow people to leave easily. Horse-drawn travel, barn raising and plain clothing all keep community life close and equal. Mobile phones may be allowed for business purposes only, kept outside the home. Tractors may be used with steel wheels to slow the work and keep it community-focused.

What happens during Rumspringa, and why does it matter?

Teenagers (roughly ages 16–20) experience life outside the community before deciding whether to be baptised and join permanently. It makes baptism a freely chosen commitment.

Why do the Amish use horse and buggy instead of cars?

Cars allow fast, independent travel away from the community. Horse and buggy keeps travel slow, local and community-focused — it does not encourage individualism.

Why is adult baptism so important to the Amish?

Faith must be freely chosen, not inherited. Baptism only happens after Rumspringa, when the person has decided for themselves to commit to the community and its values.

Why do the Amish wear plain, uniform clothing?

To avoid pride and competition over fashion. Uniform dress signals equal commitment to community over individual identity — no one stands out.
