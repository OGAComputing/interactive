Knowledge

Politics is about how countries make decisions and who is in charge. The USA is a democracy — citizens choose their leaders by voting. It has a two-party system dominated by the Republicans (sometimes called the GOP) and the Democrats. The USA has a presidential system: the President is elected directly by voters. This is different from the UK's parliamentary system, where the Prime Minister must lead the winning party in Parliament. Compare how the USA and UK political systems work.
Knowledge

The USA has a presidential system. The President is chosen by the whole country voting directly every four years and can serve a maximum of two terms. The President runs the government separately from Congress (the US equivalent of Parliament).

The UK has a parliamentary system. Voters choose MPs (Members of Parliament) in their local area. The party that wins the most seats then picks the Prime Minister — the public never vote for the PM directly. There is no fixed limit on how long a Prime Minister can serve.

The USA has a single written Constitution dating from 1787. The UK has no single written document — instead it relies on laws, traditions, and conventions that have built up over centuries.

Knowledge

USA — religion in political life: Around 63% of Americans identify as Christian (Pew Research, 2021). Many voters consider a politician's faith important. US Presidents traditionally swear the oath of office on the Bible. Politicians frequently reference God in speeches. Religious groups actively campaign on laws relating to abortion, same-sex marriage, and education.

USA — religion and the law: The First Amendment of the Constitution states that "Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion." This is called the separation of church and state. The government cannot create an official religion.

UK — religion and politics: The British monarch is the head of the Church of England (the state church). Twenty-six Church of England bishops sit in the House of Lords and can vote on new laws. MPs can choose either a religious oath or a non-religious affirmation when they join Parliament.

Sort arguments into two sides of the debate: should religion influence politics?
Knowledge

In favour of religion influencing politics: Many argue that religion provides a strong moral framework for laws and decisions. In the USA, where 63% of citizens are Christian, politicians' religious values often reflect the values of their voters. Faith communities can raise awareness of important issues and push for positive social change, such as helping the poor.

Against religion influencing politics: Critics argue that laws must apply equally to everyone — regardless of their faith or lack of it. The US Constitution already requires separation of church and state. Different Christian groups often strongly disagree with each other on issues like abortion and same-sex marriage, meaning that using religion to make laws can create conflict rather than resolve it.
