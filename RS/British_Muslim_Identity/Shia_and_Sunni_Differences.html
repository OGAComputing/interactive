What are the key differences between Shia and Sunni Muslims?

Follow one Ummah as it reaches a fork in the road, branches into Sunni and Shia — then discover how much the two still share.

Understand what caused the split in Islam, and how it shaped the way Sunni and Shia Muslims practise their faith today.
Key words for this lesson
Ummah
The worldwide community, or "global family", of all Muslims.
Denomination
A smaller group or branch within a larger religion.
Sunni
The largest branch of Islam; its first leader was chosen by the community.
Shia
The branch that believed the leader should come from the Prophet's family.
Caliph
The Sunni title for the leader who succeeds the Prophet (PBUH).
Imam
The Shia title for a spiritual leader they believe is guided by God.
Sunnah
The teachings and example of the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH).
Hijrah
The journey from Makkah to Medina in 622 CE — the start of the Islamic calendar.

Before you begin…

"Ummah" means the one global family of all Muslims. If every Muslim belongs to a single Ummah, why do you think there are different groups, or "denominations", within Islam? Pick the idea closest to your thinking — there are no marks, it's just to get you started.

Just like Christianity has Catholics and Protestants, Islam has branches too. These differences usually began over leadership (who should lead?) and interpretation (how should we practise our faith?) — not over God or the Qur'an. Work through the steps below to see exactly how it happened.

632 CE · The question
Read: what caused the split?

The year 632 CE: a turning point

In 632 CE, the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) died in the city of Medina. He had been the religious leader of Islam and the leader of the whole Muslim community, the Ummah.

After his death, Muslims faced an important question: who should lead the community now? The Prophet (PBUH) had not clearly said who the next leader should be, and Muslims disagreed about the answer.

This disagreement — at first mostly peaceful — slowly became permanent. It led to the two main branches of Islam: Sunni and Shia.
