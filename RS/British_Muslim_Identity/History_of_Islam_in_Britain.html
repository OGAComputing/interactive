Before you set off…

How long do you think Muslims have been part of Britain's population? Make your best guess — there are no marks for this, it's just to see how the journey surprises you.

The presence of Islam in Britain stretches back over 1,200 years. Many people think it only arrived in the 1950s after World War II — but that is only the most recent, most visible part of a much longer story. Set off through the timeline below to see the whole picture.
1
700s – 1600s
Early Arrivals & the Silver Link
King Offa's coin (774 AD)

King Offa of Mercia minted a gold coin with the Islamic declaration of faith (Shahada) on it — copying the trusted Islamic gold dinar. Evidence of trade links over 1,200 years ago.

Queen Elizabeth I

Elizabeth I formed alliances with the Ottoman Empire and Morocco to trade silk, spices and gunpowder — and as political strategy against enemies like Spain.

A Moroccan envoy (1600)

Abd el-Ouahed ben Messaoud, a Moroccan ambassador, visited England in 1600 as part of Elizabeth I's trade and political links with the Muslim world.

2
1700s – 1900s
The British Empire & Colonial Connections
Millions of subjects

As the Empire expanded into India, Africa and the Middle East, millions of Muslims became British subjects.

Abdul Karim

Many Muslims served in Britain. Abdul Karim became a famous teacher and confidant to Queen Victoria.

The first mosque

Before purpose-built mosques, Muslims prayed in converted houses and rented rooms — adapting the spaces they had.

3
1914 – 1960s
World Wars & the Post-WW2 Boom
Post-war migration

After 1945 Britain needed to rebuild, so workers were encouraged to come from Commonwealth countries.

Industrial hubs

Large communities settled in the "Mill Towns" of the North and Midlands to work in the textile and steel industries.

Stop 3Roughly how many soldiers from across the British Empire — many of them Muslim — fought for Britain in the First World War?

Over 400,000. Many people don't know this, because British history has often been taught in a Eurocentric way, and Commonwealth soldiers were recorded in broad groups like the "Indian Army" without their faith being noted. This history moves the story from "you arrived recently" to "you helped build and defend this country".

1970s – today
Modern Britain
Diverse communities

British Muslims are not one single group — they include people of South Asian, Arab, African, European and Caribbean heritage.

21st-century life

From the first Muslim MP in 1997 to a Mayor of London, Muslims are active in every part of British life.
