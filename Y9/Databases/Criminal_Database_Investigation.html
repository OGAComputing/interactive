1 of 4
Table
The table is the entire grid — a named collection of related data arranged in rows and columns. A database can hold many tables. This one is tbl_persons; CRIMDB also has tbl_devices, tbl_social_media and others.
↑ The whole cyan-bordered grid above is one TABLE
2 of 4
Record
A record is one complete horizontal row in a table. It holds every piece of data about a single person or item. Each row is a separate record — the highlighted green row is Maria Burton's.
↑ The green-highlighted row is one RECORD
3 of 4
Field
A field is one vertical column in a table. It stores a single category of information across every record. The highlighted amber column is the "Full Name" field — it holds a name for every person in the database.
↑ The amber-highlighted column is one FIELD
4 of 4
Primary Key
Every record needs a primary key — a unique identifier that belongs to that record alone. No two records in the same table can share a primary key. Maria's is P-1042; it belongs to her and no one else. The glowing cell in the Record ID column is her primary key value.
↑ "P-1042" in the Record ID column is the PRIMARY KEY for Maria's record
