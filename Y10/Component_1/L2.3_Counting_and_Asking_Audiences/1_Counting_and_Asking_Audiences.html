Counting and Asking Audiences

Two ways to find out about an audience: count them with real statistics, then ask them yourself.

2

Read the numbers

🎯 Three real tables of audience statistics. Answer each question from its table: type the number, or pick an answer.

💡 How to work it out

"How much more?" Take the smaller number away from the bigger one: 56.9 − 37.6.

Percentages: 63% means 63 in every 100. For a class of 30: 30 × 63 ÷ 100, then round to a whole person.

"How many times as many?" Divide the bigger number by the smaller one: 850,000 ÷ 40,000.

"What does it NOT tell you?" A statistic counts people or money. It doesn't say who they are or why they chose it.

3

Find two statistics

🎯 Find two real audience statistics for two products in your research log from Lesson 2.1 (it opens in a new tab). For each one: the number, the link to the page, who made the page, and what the number tells you.

💡 Where to look

Films: search "[film] UK box office" and look for the BFI or a news site such as BBC News.

Newspapers and magazines: search "[title] circulation ABC" (Press Gazette reports the ABC figures).

YouTube, TikTok, Instagram: the channel's or account's own page shows its subscribers or followers. Write the date you looked: these numbers change every day.

Games, music and books: search "[product] copies sold" and look for the company's own news, or a trusted news site.

Example: "A Minecraft Movie took £56.9 million at UK and Ireland cinemas in 2025." This box office figure shows that millions of people paid to see it, so it had a very big audience.

Which way to ask?

InterviewYou ask one person questions and listen to their answers in detail.
Focus groupA small group try the product together, then discuss it while you ask questions.
SurveyThe same questions for lots of people, so you can count the answers.
Multiple choicePick one of a few answers.
Rating scaleAnswer with a number, like 1 to 5.
RankingPut things in order, favourite first.
Open-endedAnswer in your own words.

A follow-up question asks for more about the answer someone just gave. Your sample is the people you choose to ask.

💡 What makes a good survey

Fair: the question doesn't push people towards one answer.

One thing at a time: two questions in one get muddled answers.

The right sample: enough people, from the product's target audience, who are different from each other.

Interview a partner

Pick the product from your research log (it opens in a new tab).

🎯 Plan four questions, then record a 2-minute audio interview with your partner about the product. Swap, so you both get interviewed. Then write up what they said.

  1. Plan: fill in the four question boxes below.
  2. Set up: sit side by side, show your partner the product (its poster, trailer or home page), and put them at ease.
  3. Record: in PowerPoint, Insert › Audio › Record Audio…, name it Interview – your name, then press the red record button.
  4. Ask: your four questions. Listen, and ask your follow-up when an answer is interesting. Stop at about 2 minutes (watch the timer), then click OK.
  5. Save: right-click the speaker icon › Save Media as…, and save it in your OneDrive. Upload it to this lesson's Classroom assignment.
