Genres Change

Genres don't stand still. Smaller sub-genres grow inside them, two genres get mixed into hybrids, and producers break the rules on purpose to surprise us.

Warm-up · optional
✓

Sub-genre or hybrid?

Sub-genrePart of a genre: a smaller group inside it. Zombie films are a sub-genre of horror.
HybridTwo genres mixed: it has the conventions of both. A horror comedy is a hybrid.

🎯 Each card is a made-up product. Does it stay inside one genre (a sub-genre), or mix two (a hybrid)? Four cards in each box.

Done? Now try Task 1: six real products.

1

Inside or mixed?

Sub-genrePart of a genre: a smaller group inside one genre.
HybridTwo genres mixed: it has the conventions and iconography of both.

🎯 Six real products. For each one, is it a sub-genre or a hybrid, and of which genre or genres? Answer all six, then check.

💡 How to decide

Ask: "Is it still one genre, just a smaller, more exact group of it?" Then it's a sub-genre. A zombie film is still a horror film.

Ask: "Can I see the conventions or iconography of two genres?" Then it's a hybrid. A horror comedy has the monsters of horror and the jokes of comedy.

Use the words

🎯 Describe one sub-genre product and one hybrid product in your own words. Use a product from Task 1, or one you know. Full sentences: your teacher reads these.

💡 An example, for two different products

Sub-genre: Just Dance is a sub-genre of music games because you score points by keeping to the beat of real songs, like every music game, but you do it by dancing in front of the screen.

Hybrid: Scooby-Doo is a hybrid of horror and comedy because it has haunted houses and monsters from horror, and a talking dog who makes silly jokes from comedy.

Repeat, add or subvert?

RepetitionIt follows a convention of its genre, so we get what we expect.
DifferenceIt adds something new to its genre: something original.
SubvertDo the opposite: it breaks a convention on purpose.

🎯 Watch the three clips, and read the conventions of each product's genre. Then sort the cards: is each one repetition, difference or subverting? Three cards in each box.

Encanto (2021)

A Disney animated musical. Its conventions:

  • songs about how the characters feel
  • a hero on a journey
  • a villain to defeat
  • a happy ending
Horrible Histories (CBBC, since 2009)

A history programme for children. Its conventions:

  • true facts about the past
  • a calm, serious presenter
  • real people and places from history
Untitled Goose Game (2019)

A stealth game. Its conventions:

  • sneak and hide so nobody sees you
  • a list of goals to tick off
  • you play a spy or a hero
💡 How to decide

Find the card's product, then read its list of conventions.

The card matches something in the list → repetition. The card does the opposite of something in the list → subverting. The card is nothing like anything in the list → difference.

Then and now

🎯 Three pairs: the same genre before 2000 and today. Pick one pair. What does the new one repeat, what is different, and which convention does it subvert? Full sentences: your teacher reads these.

Pokémon: role-playing games

Batman: superhero

Godzilla: monster films

💡 An example, for a different pair: The Legend of Zelda (1986) and Tears of the Kingdom (2023)

Repetition: Both are action-adventure games. Tears of the Kingdom repeats the conventions of the 1986 game: you play Link, explore Hyrule with a sword and shield, and fight Ganon.

Difference: The biggest difference is the huge 3D world with islands in the sky, where you build your own machines, which makes it feel new because every player solves a puzzle their own way.

Subverting: The old game makes Zelda a princess who waits to be rescued, but Tears of the Kingdom subverts this: Zelda travels back in time and turns herself into a dragon to save Hyrule.

Photoshop: bring it up to date

🎯 Take a poster from before 2000 and add today's iconography, so it looks like this year's remake. Keep its genre: change the iconography, not the conventions. About 15 minutes.

  1. Open the old poster in Photoshop (File › Open): the Godzilla (1954) or Batman (1966) poster from the course Drive folder › Images (godzilla_1954.jpg or batman_1966.jpg).
  2. Make it a layer: in the Layers panel, double-click Background and call it Old poster.
  3. Add today: File › Place Embedded… a picture of something from today (a phone, a drone, a city skyline at night) from the Photo Bank, Pexels or Unsplash. Drag the corners to size it, then press Enter.
  4. Name every layer: double-click each new layer's name and say what it is: Phone, Drone, New title.
  5. Order them: drag layers up or down the Layers panel; the top layer is in front.
  6. Group them: Ctrl+click your new layers, press Ctrl+G, and call the group Today.
  7. Export: File › Export › Export As… JPG. Take a screenshot of your Layers panel (Windows + Shift + S). Upload both to Classroom.
