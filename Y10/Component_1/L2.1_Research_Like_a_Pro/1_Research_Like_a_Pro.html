Research Like a Pro

Find out about media products the way a researcher does: ask people yourself, check who you can trust, and keep a log of where everything came from.

Primary or secondary?

Primary researchResearch you do yourself. You collect new information first-hand: you ask people, watch them, or talk it through.
Secondary researchResearch someone else has already done. You find it and use it: websites, articles, reviews, videos, books.

1

Which research?

SurveyThe same questions asked to lots of people.
InterviewLots of questions asked to one person, with their answers recorded.
ObservationWatching what people do and noting it down.
DiscussionA group talks it through while you note what each person says.
Secondary researchSomething someone else made: a website, article, review or video.

🎯 Questions 1–5: name the research. Questions 6–8: pick the research that would actually find the answer. Answer all eight, then check.

💡 How to decide

First ask: did you collect it yourself? If not, it's secondary research.

If you did: lots of people, same questions? Survey. One person, lots of questions? Interview. Watching, not asking? Observation. A group talking together? Discussion.

For 6–8: facts and figures (dates, money, sales) are already written down somewhere: look them up. What people think you usually have to ask them.

Trust it?

Who made it?Official or a trusted name? Or anonymous? Could they be biased?

💡 Clues

Run the four checks on each card. A card that passes all four goes near Trust it. A card that fails one but that you could check (follow its link, find a newer figure) goes in the middle. An anonymous post, a bare opinion or a claim with no source goes near Don't trust it.

AI chatbots can make a claim sound true even when it's made up. Always fact-check what they say.

Spot the slip

Research logA record of your research: each source, what you found, and when.
ReferenceWhere it came from: who made it, what it is, the link, and the date you looked.
PlagiarismUsing someone else's words or work as if they were yours. Use quotation marks and a reference instead.
Screenshot · screen recordingA picture or a video of your screen, as evidence. Windows + Shift + S takes a screenshot; PowerPoint's Insert › Screen Recording records video.

Start your research log

🎯 Real research, now. Find each fact online, from a source you trust. Paste the link to the page you found it on, and say who made that page. Then do one piece of primary research: ask someone near you. When it's all done, your references appear at the bottom.

💡 How to find a fact and copy its link

Search for the product and the question. Open a page from a source you trust: don't stop at the search results. Find the fact on that page.

Click the address bar at the top of the browser, press Ctrl + C to copy the link, then click the link box here and press Ctrl + V.

Who made the page? Look at the logo or the name at the top: BBC News, Netflix, Beano.com…
