Component 1 · Exploring Media ProductsLesson 1.3

Who Made It and Why?

Every media product has a producer. Who are they, what is the product for, and what do they get out of it?

1

Mystery producer

Media conglomerateA giant company that owns lots of media businesses: film studios, TV channels, music, games.
Public service broadcasterA broadcaster with a duty to serve the whole public: to inform, educate and entertain, not just make money.
Independent producerA person or small company that makes its own products and isn't owned by a media giant.
Community mediaMade by and for a local area or group, not for profit, often by volunteers.

🎯 Seven real producers. Each clue tells you something about how the producer works: who owns it, how it's paid for, how big it is. Name its type as early as you dare. Guess on clue 1 for ★★★. A wrong guess loses gold and makes you wait.

💡 How to decide

Does it own lots of other media businesses? Conglomerate. Does it have a duty to the whole public, and get its money from the licence fee or the public? Public service broadcaster. Is it one person or a small team that no big company owns? Independent. Is it run for one local area or group, not for profit? Community media.

2

Spot the slip

PurposeWhat the product does for the audience: entertain, inform, educate, persuade, inspire or challenge.
MotivationWhy the producer makes it: to generate profit, raise awareness, benefit a community, for self-expression, innovation, or public service responsibilities.
AimWhat the producer wants the product to be like: high quality, distinctive, accessible, diverse, inclusive or impartial.

Worked model · McDonald's McCrispy billboard, 2025

Purpose · for the audience

To persuade. "Betray your go-to" tells you to swap your usual order for a McCrispy.

Motivation · for the producer

To generate profit. Every person who swaps buys a burger, so McDonald's makes money.

Aim · what it should be like

Distinctive. Bright yellow, one huge burger and three words: you can tell it's McDonald's from across the street.

Watch out: "to make money" is a motivation, not a purpose. The audience doesn't get the money, the producer does.

💡 What to check in each sentence

Is the type of producer right? (Look back at Task 1.) Is a purpose really something the audience gets (a laugh, some facts, a reason to buy)? Is a motivation something the producer wants or gets (money, a message heard, their own idea made)? Is an aim about what the product is like (expensive-looking, fair, easy for everyone to use)?

3

Name the reason

🎯 Each question describes a real product. Pick the exact word for its purpose, motivation or aim. Answer all eight, then check.

💡 The words

Motivations: generate profit (make money) · raise awareness (make people know about something) · benefit a community (help a local area or group) · self-expression (make your own idea) · innovation (do something new) · public service responsibilities (a duty to serve everyone).

Purposes: entertain · inform (give facts) · educate (teach) · persuade (get you to do or buy something) · inspire (make you feel you could do it too) · challenge (question an idea people take for granted).

Aims: high quality · distinctive (stands out, unlike anything else) · accessible (easy for everyone to get and use) · diverse (shows many different kinds of people) · inclusive (makes everyone feel welcome) · impartial (fair to every side).

💡 Finding the producer, and a full example

Not sure who made it? Search "product name made by", or look at the end credits, the app store page or the magazine's contents page.

Stuck on which is which? Ask who gets it? If the audience gets it, it's a purpose. If the producer gets it, it's a motivation. What the product is like is an aim.

Producer and purposeStardew Valley was made by ConcernedApe, which is an independent producer. Its purpose is to entertain because you relax by running your own farm at your own pace.
MotivationThe motivation of ConcernedApe is self-expression because one man spent four years making the farming game he wanted to play.
AimOne aim was to make it accessible. You can see this because it is cheap and you can play it on a phone, a console or a PC.
