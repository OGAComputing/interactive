Telling Stories

Every story can be told in more than one order. Film-makers choose a structure to hook us, and news writers put the most important facts first.

Warm-up · optional
✓

Which structure?

LinearThe events in the order they happen, from start to finish.
Non-linearJumps about in time: it might start at the end, then go back.
CircularEnds where it began: the same place, person or moment.

Story order

StoryEvery event, in order: even the ones we never see.
PlotThe order we're shown: the events as the film shows them.

Wicked tells the life of Elphaba, a girl born with green skin, who grows up to be the Wicked Witch of the West. Her roommate, Galinda, later calls herself Glinda. You don't need to have seen it: every card has a clue to when it happens.

🎯 Wicked (2024) doesn't show its events in the order they happen. Put its seven scenes in story order: what happens first goes at the top. Drag a card, or use its ▲ ▼ buttons.

💡 How to decide

Look for the time words on each card: first day, for weeks, at last, now, once. Ask: "When does this happen in Elphaba's life?"

The film opens with the party for Elphaba's death. She is grown up by then, and everyone calls her the Wicked Witch. So when does that party happen in her story?

2

Name the structure

LinearIn the order it happens, start to finish.
Non-linearJumps about in time.
CircularEnds where it began.
InteractiveThe audience's choices change what happens.
Open endingLeft unsolved: we don't know what happens next.
Closed endingEvery question is answered: it's finished.
Single-strandOne storyline.
Multi-strandSeveral storylines at once.

🎯 Seven real products. Read about each one, then choose the structure it uses. Answer all seven, then check.

💡 Which question is it asking?

Some questions are about time (linear, non-linear or circular), some about the ending (open or closed), some about how many storylines (single- or multi-strand), and one is about who chooses what happens.

Tell it out of order

🎯 Producers make a plot non-linear to hook the audience from the first minute. Take a linear film or show you know (Moana 2, or one of your own) and plan a non-linear plot for it.

💡 Stuck for an idea?

Open with the most exciting moment, near the end of the story: a storm, a chase, a big fight. Then jump back to show how the hero got there. The audience keeps watching to find out.

3

Build the pyramid

Inverted pyramidMost important first. The top says what happened, who, where and when; details come next; background goes at the bottom.

🎯 Watch the clip first: Neil Armstrong becomes the first person to walk on the Moon. Below is a news story from that morning, 21 July 1969, with its paragraphs scrambled. Put them back in inverted-pyramid order: most important at the top.

💡 How to decide

Ask of each paragraph: "If the reader stopped here, what would they miss?" The top paragraph on its own must tell the whole news.

Some paragraphs could fairly swap places: each card is marked by whether it is in a fair place.

4

Use the words

🎯 Use today's words to describe real products: a product from this lesson, or one you know. Full sentences: your teacher reads these.

💡 An example, for products not in this lesson's tasks

Story and plot: In Toy Story (1995), the story and the plot are in the same order, because the film never jumps back: it shows Woody meeting Buzz and then their adventure, as it happens.

Structure: EastEnders is multi-strand, because each episode cuts between the storylines of different families in Albert Square.

Inverted pyramid: A news story about a cup final uses an inverted pyramid: the winner and the score come first, because that's what most readers want to know.
