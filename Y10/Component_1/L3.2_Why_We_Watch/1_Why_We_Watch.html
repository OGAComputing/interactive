Why We Watch

Nobody makes you watch, play or listen. You choose a product because it gives you something you need, and two people can need very different things from the same product.

Why are they watching?

Uses and gratificationsA theory: the audience is active. People choose media to meet their own needs, and the need met is their gratification (reward).

🎯 Four real people, four real products. Each clue tells you more about why that person uses it. Name the need as early as you dare. Guess on clue 1 for ★★★. A wrong guess loses gold and makes you wait.

💡 How to decide

Are they finding something out? Information. Just having fun? Entertainment. Getting away from real life? Escapism. Showing who they are? Personal identity. Sharing or talking with other people? Social interaction.

It's about the person, not the product: wait for the clue that tells you why they use it.

One product, two needs

🎯 Pick one product from your diary. Explain the need it meets for you, a different need it meets for someone else, and what the producer has put in it to meet one of them.

💡 An example, for Planet Earth III

For you: For me, Planet Earth III meets a need for information because I learn how animals survive in the desert.

4

Two thumbnails, two needs

ThumbnailThe small picture that sells a video before you click. Producers design it to promise a need: a laugh, an escape, something to talk about.

🎯 Pick one starting picture, Planet Earth III or Paddington in Peru, and make two thumbnails from it. Each one must promise a different need. Use adjustment layers, a title, and a cut-out from another picture. About 25 minutes.

  1. Open your starting picture from the Drive folder's Images (File › Open): planet_earth_iii_thumb.jpg or paddington_in_peru_thumb.jpg. Decide the need your first thumbnail will promise.
  2. Colour: add at least two adjustment layers (Layer › New Adjustment Layer › Brightness/Contrast, Vibrance, Hue/Saturation, Photo Filter or Gradient Map). Bright and bold, or dark and dreamy?
  3. Cut-out: File › Place Embedded… a second picture from the Drive folder's Photo Bank › Cut-outs (animals and faces). Select it with the Object Selection Tool (W) or Select › Subject, then Layer › Layer Mask › Reveal Selection. Resize it with Ctrl + T.
  4. Title: pick one from the tag lines below (or write your own), then add it with the Type Tool (T). Make it big; add an outline with Layer › Layer Style › Stroke.
  5. Export: File › Export › Export As… JPG, named thumbnail 1. Screenshot the Layers panel (Windows + Shift + S).
  6. Thumbnail 2: change the adjustment layers, title and cut-out so it promises a different need. Export it as thumbnail 2 and screenshot the Layers panel again.
  7. Upload both JPGs and both screenshots to Classroom, then answer the questions below.

🏷️ Tag lines to pick from. Don't spend long on the words: choose one for each thumbnail and get on with Photoshop.

💡 Which look promises which need?

Entertainment: bright, saturated colours, a bold title with "!", a funny or surprised cut-out.

Escapism: soft, dreamy colours (Gradient Map, Photo Filter), lower contrast, a calm title.

Information: clear, natural colours, a title that asks a question or promises facts.

Social interaction: a title that speaks to "you" and friends; a cut-out face reacting.

Personal identity: a title about who you are ("For true nature fans"), a look fans would recognise.
