Reading an Image

A poster shows us things, suggests ideas, and uses words to tell us which idea to take.

1

Jaws: see it, read it

DenotationWhat you can actually see or hear. Just describe it.
ConnotationWhat it suggests: the ideas and feelings it gives you.
AnchorageWords that fix the meaning, so we read the image the way the producer wants.
PolysemyAn image can be read in more than one way. Anchorage picks one reading for us.
EncodingThe producer chooses every detail on purpose to put a meaning in. We "decode" it when we read it.

Worked model · Jaws poster, 1975

1 · Denotation

A woman swims at the top of the sea. A huge shark rises straight up towards her with its mouth open.

2 · Connotation

She is relaxed and has no idea what is below her. Danger is coming, and we know before she does: that makes us tense.

3 · Anchorage

"The terrifying motion picture" fixes the meaning: this is a horror film made to scare us, not a nature documentary about sharks.

4 · Encoding

None of this is an accident. The designers chose a red title, a tiny swimmer and a shark she can't see to encode danger, so we decode it as horror.

🎯 Now you. Sort these 8 statements about the same poster: is each one denotation or connotation?

💡 How to tell them apart

Ask: could two people argue about it? If not (it's just there on the poster), it's denotation. If it's an idea or a feeling the poster gives you, it's connotation. Words like suggests, hints and feels point to connotation.

2

Words that fix meaning

PolysemyAn image can be read in more than one way. A photo of someone running: late, scared, or training?
AnchorageA caption, title or tagline picks one reading for us and "anchors" it in place.

Look at the small caption beside Ken: "She's everything. He's just Ken."

Without it, the picture shows two smiling people on a pink letter. With it, we know whose film this is.

Photoshop: one photo, two captions

🎯 Use polysemy and anchorage: one photo can mean many things, so make it mean two different things just by changing the caption.

1. Pick your photo

Download it from the course Photo Bank ↗ (free Pexels photos).

2 · In Photoshop

  1. File › Open your photo.
  2. Pick the Horizontal Type Tool (T), click on the photo and type caption 1. Change the font and size in the Options bar at the top.
  3. File › Export › Export As…, choose JPG, and save it as caption1.
  4. Double-click the T on the text layer in the Layers panel, type caption 2 over it, and export again as caption2.
  5. Attach both JPGs to this lesson's Classroom assignment.

3 · Preview and explain

💡 Example

The same photo of someone running: "Late for the last bus" makes it everyday and a bit funny. "Running for his life" makes it scary. Short captions work best: look at real newspaper captions.
