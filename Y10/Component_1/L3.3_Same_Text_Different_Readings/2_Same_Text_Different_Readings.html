Passive or active?

Passive audienceTakes the media in without doing anything back or questioning it.
Active audienceDoes something with it: votes, replies, makes things, or questions what it says.

The panic that wasn't

Passive audienceTakes the message in, without questioning it. An old idea said every audience is passive: one message goes into every head, and everyone reacts the same way.

The story

  • 1On 30 October 1938, American radio played War of the Worlds, a drama about Martians landing in New Jersey.
  • 2It sounded like real news: music kept being cut off by bulletins saying the Martians were attacking.
  • 3The next morning, newspapers said thousands of terrified listeners had run from their homes.
  • 4Researchers later found most people weren't listening at all, and most who were knew it was a play.
  • 5Some listeners were scared. Others checked another station or a newspaper, and calmed down.

🎯 Read the story, then answer all five questions and check.

💡 What to look for

If the audience is passive, the message goes straight in and everybody reacts the same way. If it is active, people question it, check it, or do something with it.

Ask: did everyone react the same way to War of the Worlds?

Three readings

Preferred readingThe producer's meaning: the audience takes the message the way the producer wanted.
Negotiated readingThe audience accepts some of the producer's meaning, but not all of it.
Oppositional readingRejects the message: the audience disagrees with what the producer meant.

The advert

  • 1In 1995 Coca-Cola's red trucks, covered in lights, first drove through snowy towns to the song "Holidays Are Coming".
  • 2In 2024 Coca-Cola remade the advert using generative AI instead of filming it.
  • 3Coca-Cola's message: when the trucks arrive, Christmas has begun, and Coca-Cola is part of it.
  • 4Lots of viewers posted about it online, and they did not all agree.

🎯 These comments are from imaginary viewers. Which reading is each one? Three cards in each box. Click a card, then click its box (or drag it).

💡 How to decide

Does the viewer agree with Coca-Cola's message, all of it? Preferred.

Do they agree with part of it, but have a "but…"? Negotiated.

Do they reject the message, or what the advert stands for? Oppositional.

Snapper: one advert, three readings

The advert

  • 1John Lewis's Christmas advert for 2023, shown on TV, YouTube and social media.
  • 2A boy grows a Venus flytrap, called Snapper, to be his family's Christmas tree.
  • 3Snapper grows huge and causes chaos, so the family put it outside. At Christmas they gather round it: it is part of the family, because the boy loves it.
  • 4The advert's message is that Christmas traditions can change and grow.
  • 5Many families wait for the John Lewis advert every November, and the papers review it.
  • 6Some viewers loved how funny and odd it was. Others said it was not as moving as older John Lewis adverts, like The Man on the Moon (2015).

🎯 Write one reading of Snapper in each box, then say whether its viewers were one passive audience. Full sentences: your teacher reads these.

💡 An example, for Coca-Cola's trucks

Preferred: The preferred reading is that Christmas starts when the Coca-Cola trucks arrive, so Coke belongs at a family Christmas.

Passive or active? A passive audience would all take in that message, but many viewers rejected the AI advert, so they were not passive.
