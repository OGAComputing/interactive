Who Is It For?

Every media product is made for someone. Work out who, from the evidence: their age, gender and socio-economic group, then how they live.

Age, gender or group?

AgeHow old the audience is: an age range, like 8 to 12, or "in their twenties".
GenderWhether the audience is mostly male, mostly female, or a mix.
Socio-economic groupThe kind of jobs the audience does, and so how much money they have to spend.

1

Who's in the audience?

Primary audienceThe main group a product is made for. Also called the target audience.
Secondary audienceOther people it appeals to as well, though it isn't mainly made for them.
A/BSenior managers and professionals: lawyers, head teachers, architects.
C1Office, admin and supervisor jobs: bank clerks, call-centre staff.
C2Skilled trades: plumbers, mechanics, hairdressers.
D/EManual jobs (packing, cleaning), and people on a state pension or not working.

Socio-economic groups sort people by their job. Advertisers often join them into two: ABC1 (A, B and C1) and C2DE (C2, D and E).

🎯 Answer all nine questions, then check.

💡 How to decide

Primary or secondary? Ask: who did the producer make it for? That's the primary audience. Anyone else who enjoys it is a secondary audience.

Which group? Match the job to the nearest example in the boxes above. A job that needs years of training and runs things is A/B; a desk job is C1; a trade you train for with your hands is C2.

Gender: "mostly" is safer than "only". Very few products have no fans of the other gender.

2

Who's it for?

🎯 Four real products, one at a time. Read the clue, then pick its primary audience: age · gender · socio-economic group. Guess on the first clue for ★★★. A wrong guess shows another clue and makes you wait.

💡 What to look for

Age: what is it about, and who are its stars? When is it on?

Gender: are boys and girls (or men and women) both in it, or mostly one?

Socio-economic group: what does it cost, and what do its adverts sell? Cheap or free → all groups. Expensive, or adverts for luxury brands → mostly ABC1. On while people are at work → people who are retired or not working, so mostly C2DE.

3

A football fan

A lifestyle profile describes how one typical member of the audience lives. The four parts, with the examples from the board:

InterestsWhat they like doing or knowing about. EA FC, football shirts, transfer news.
AttitudesWhat they think or feel about things: opinions. Thinks their team's manager should be sacked.
ValuesWhat matters most to them: big ideas. Belonging: the club is like a family.
BehaviourWhat they actually do: habits you could see. Goes to every home game.

🎯 Jordan, 34, is a typical football fan. Put each fact about Jordan in the right part of the profile: two cards in each box.

💡 How to tell them apart

An interest is a topic or hobby. An attitude is an opinion: it often starts "thinks…" or "feels…".

A value is a big idea that matters most: loyalty, family, fairness, freedom. Behaviour is something you could watch them do, or count.

4

Profile the audience

🎯 Build an audience profile for two real products, one from each sector here. Read each evidence file, then fill in every field of the card. Check when both are done.

💡 Use the evidence

Age: who does the producer say it's for? What's the age rating? Gender: look for survey figures. Socio-economic group: what does it cost? Free → all groups.

Secondary audience: people outside the primary audience who still use it. The primary audience itself is never the answer.

Lifestyle: each statement is right in only one field. Is it a topic (interest), an opinion (attitude), a big idea (value) or a habit (behaviour)?
